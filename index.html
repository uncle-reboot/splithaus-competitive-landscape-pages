Competitive analysis

Shared-home competitors.

Who they are, when they started, what they charge, and what to test.

Reviewed: 23–24 September 2026. Public vendor claims and list prices, not hands-on product tests or checkout quotes. “Not verified” means we did not find a reliable public source; it does not mean the feature or company is absent.

Executive finding

  • The direct contest: software for families who already share a vacation home. Test rivals against one real seasonal scheduling scenario.61913
  • Do not claim “only fair calendar.” Rivals document round-robin queues, seasonal rotations and share-weighted scheduling.61913
  • Best testable angle: can an existing family set its own rules, allocate peak dates, explain the result and adapt when plans change—with less work for its organizer?15 This is a hypothesis, not a proven advantage.

1. SplitHaus baseline

  • Who it serves: an already shared family home, often 3–8 households planning a season together.5
  • Founders in the reviewed internal record: Matt Wells and Dan (surname not verified).5
  • Product evidence: public site describes calendar, turn-taking/draft, preference-and-share Match, changes and expenses.1 Internal notes describe more flows at source level but do not establish production reliability.5
  • Price caution: public site says $119/house/year after one free year; internal billing and claims records conflict. Do not reuse that price as approved marketing copy until reconciled.15

4. No-new-app substitutes

  • Calendar + spreadsheet + group chat: low incremental cost; test whether SplitHaus saves the family organizer time this season.5152
  • Splitwise: established expense sharing; do not sell expenses alone as differentiation.53
  • Cozi / Notion / Airtable: familiar family calendar or flexible databases. Compare the time needed to encode a real household allocation policy, not feature-list length.545560

5. What to test next

  • Same-household trial: give each direct rival a 3–8-household case with shares, holiday preferences and last year's priority. Time setup and allocation; record unsupported rules without calling them impossible.5
  • Changed-plan trial: introduce one cancellation, one disputed edit and a relative who does not use email. Can the organizer explain and revise the result?5
  • Adoption gate: observe the organizer's setup, a second household joining, first seasonal allocation and next-season reuse; public vendor pages do not prove conversion or retention.54
  • Before any comparative claim: run SplitHaus booking-integrity tests and reconcile billing/free-year and expenses scope. Do not assert fairer or more reliable without results.51

Evidence limits: Prices and features are vendor-documented; no accounts, demos, purchases or customer interviews were used. Founding-year provenance is separate from a product's first public appearance. Unknown is not absent.
